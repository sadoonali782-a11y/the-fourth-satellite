How GPS works in simple words

    
Every GPS satellite constantly broadcasts the exact time. Your phone compares that time with its own clock to work out how long the signal took to arrive. Multiply that delay by the speed of light and you get a distance. With distances from several satellites, the phone finds the one point where all the distance circles meet. This is called trilateration.

    
This simulator is a flat 2D model, so the numbers are scaled for easy viewing. Real GPS works in 3D.


    
Frequently asked questions

    
Why does GPS need four satellites?

    
In real 3D space the phone has four unknowns: x, y, z and its own clock error. Four satellites give four equations. In this flat model there are three unknowns, so three satellites are enough.

    
How big is a 1 microsecond clock error?

    
Light travels about 300 metres in one microsecond, so a 1 µs clock error becomes roughly 300 m of position error.

    
What is DOP?

    
Dilution of Precision (DOP) shows how satellite positions affect accuracy. Satellites spread across the sky give a low DOP. Satellites bunched together give a high DOP and larger errors.

    
Why do buildings hurt GPS?

    
Signals bounce off buildings and take a longer path. The phone then thinks the satellite is farther away. This is called multipath error.

    
What does Einstein have to do with GPS?

    
Relativity makes satellite clocks run about 38 µs per day faster than ground clocks. Without correction, GPS positions would drift by about 11 km every day.
